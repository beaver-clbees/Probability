    7.

    In summary, suppose we have a Poisson process with mean \(\lambda\text{.}\) If we let \(X\) be the continuous R.V. that measures the waiting time until the \(\alpha\)th occurrence then \(X\) has a gamma distribution with parameter \(\theta = \frac{1}{\lambda}\text{.}\) Note that since \(\lambda\) represents the average number of occurrence per unit of time, then \(\theta\) represents the expected wait time until the first occurrence. The pdf of \(X\) is

    We will use this distribution more next term in statistics but for now we will just make a few more definitions and restrict our use to learning the notation and tables.
    🔗
    The \(100p^{th}%\) percentile (for \(0\leq p \leq 1\)) is a number \(\pi_p\) such that the area under the curve of \(f(x)\) to the left of \(\pi_p\) is equal to \(p\text{.}\) That is
    \begin{equation*} p=\int_{-\infty}^{\pi_p} f(x)\, dx = F(\pi_p) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Suppose \(X\) has a chi-square distribution with \(r\) degrees of freedom. Define \(\chi^{2}_{\alpha}(r)\) so that
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} P[X\geq \chi^{2}_{\alpha}(r)]=\alpha. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    In other words, the area under the curve of \(f(x)\) to the right of \(\chi^{2}_{\alpha}(r)\) is \(\alpha\text{.}\) It follows that \(\chi^{2}_{\alpha}(r)\) is the \(100(1-\alpha)\)th percentile.
