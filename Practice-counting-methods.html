    12.

    Suppose you have \(n\) objects where \(n_{1}\) are of one type, \(n_{2}\) are of another type,...,\(n_{k}\) are of the \(k\)-th type, where \(n=n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{k}.\) How many distinguishable permutations of the objects are there? Give a formula.
    🔗
    🔗
    The above formula is called the multinomial coefficient and is denoted by \(\binom{n}{n_{1}, n_{2}, \ldots, n_{k}}.\)
