    Back to probability. Let \(N(A)\) be the number of outcomes in event \(A\text{.}\) If all outcomes in a sample space are equally likely, then the probability of an event \(A\) is \(\displaystyle{P(A)=\frac{N(A)}{N(S)}}\text{.}\) So knowing how to count helps us compute probabilities.

    Notation
    🔗
    • The number of permutations on \(n\) distinct objects taken \(r\) at a time is denoted by \(_{n}P_{r}\) or \(P(n,r)\) and is given by \(_{n}P_{r} = \frac{n!}{(n-r)!}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • The number of combinations (unordered sets) of \(n\) distinct objects taken \(r\) at a time is denoted by \(_{n}C_{r}\text{,}\) \(C(n,r)\text{,}\) or \(\binom{n}{r}\) (read "n choose r") and is given by \(_{n}C_{r} = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}\text{.}\)
